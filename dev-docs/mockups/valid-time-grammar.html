Epimemer · design note · valid time on the timeline panel

Drawing valid time

A visual grammar for validity intervals — one mark per concept in the T1 data model, and nothing drawn that the data does not assert. Concrete reads as crisp; ignorance dissolves; the absence of a boundary continues. If two things are different in the model, they must be different on screen.

The problem is older than it looks: Joseph Priestley's Chart of Biography (1765) — the chart every timeline descends from — drew certain lifespans as solid lines and uncertain ones as dots. The Information-is-Beautiful register (muted bands, one accent, annotation over ornament, whitespace doing the separating) is the modern voice of the same idea.

01 · endpoints

Three endpoint states, three treatments

T1's load-bearing distinction is unknown ≠ unbounded, so they may never share a treatment. A point gets a crisp cap. Unknown dissolves — a gradient to nothing, because we do not know where the edge is. Unbounded continues — the bar runs to the frame and leaves it, because there is no edge to find.

02 · witness points

The witness point — certainty inside the fog

The commonest real record is a claim with no usable endpoints and one witness: “asserted true at T, extent unknown”. That deserves the most recognisable mark in the grammar — a solid dot with the interval dissolving symmetrically away from it.

03 · vague labels

Labelled vagueness is not the same ignorance

“During the Renaissance” is a human assertion with soft edges — different in kind from an unknown endpoint, so it gets a different texture: a hatched band, only ever drawn once someone has explicitly resolved approximate bounds for the label. An unresolved label has no coordinates at all and must never be placed on the axis — it goes to the tray, label intact.

UNDATED TRAY · LABEL-ONLY, NO POSITION ASSERTED under the USSR? before the merger? in-universe: after the Long Night⧗

04 · per-source strips

One strip per source — the “no default collapse” rule, drawn

T1 reads validity back as (source, interval) pairs with no built-in union. The panel honours that: a fact shows thin parallel strips, one per source, each direct-labelled. If a summary is wanted, it is a hollow envelope — outline only, explicitly “any source asserts” — so it can never be mistaken for a claim someone made.

05 · provenance of the interval

Stated, inferred, proposed

Every interval is marked stated or inferred, and reflect can propose a boundary for review. Fill weight carries the tier: stated is solid, inferred is hollow with a dashed edge, and a proposed boundary is a dashed cap wearing a review chip until a human accepts it.

06 · recurrence

Beads on a spine — and a gap is not a “no”

A recurring claim is one node with several intervals: bars threaded on a hairline claim spine so they read as episodes of one thing. The spine is deliberately near-invisible, because under open-world semantics the space between bars means no assertion — never “false”. Colouring the gaps (red, grey bands, anything) would draw a claim nobody made.

07 · succession & status

Succession is a connector; status is a volume knob

temporally_followed_by is drawn as a small elbow from the end of one claim to the start of the next — order, not replacement, so a chain returning to its own lane is drawn calmly rather than treated as an error. Status reuses the #55 rule: historical stays visible at lower volume (desaturated, never hidden); corrected is hidden by default and struck-through when summoned.

08 · the vertical panel

Composed: the grammar on Epimemer’s own axis

Everything above, rotated onto the panel’s conventions — past at the top, facts left of the axis, inferences right, the timeline’s own now as a rule the bars must cross honestly. Note the active bar fading through the now-line: no source has closed it, and currency is a reading, not a stored fact.

09 · rules

Rules the grammar must not break

Each row is a place where a reasonable-looking rendering would quietly assert something the data does not say.

RuleBecause
A gap is never styled as “false” Open world: outside a stated interval is no assertion. A red or shaded gap draws a claim nobody made.
Unknown and unbounded never share a treatment They are different values in the model (T1’s load-bearing distinction); fade for one, frame-exit for the other.
A label-only interval never sits on the axis No coordinates were asserted. Placing it anywhere is fabrication — it lives in the tray until someone resolves it.
No filled union bar, ever “No default collapse” is a data rule; the summary envelope is hollow and labelled, or it is a lie in pixel form.
Historical is muted, not hidden Still true of its period — the whole reason the status exists. Hiding it re-creates the invisibility T3 just fixed.
Corrected is hidden, not deleted Mirrors include_corrected=off: summonable for “what did we believe that was wrong?”, struck-through when shown.
Cross-clock claims never share an axis Comparison across timelines is unknown by definition; an in-universe claim on the CE axis asserts a mapping nobody made. Tray, with a clock badge.
Bars fade through “now”, they do not stop at it Stopping asserts an endpoint. Currency is a reading against the timeline’s reference clock, not a stored boundary.
